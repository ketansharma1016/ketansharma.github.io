Automated Medication Dispenser

TypeMechatronics
ControllerRaspberry Pi Pico
Result90% detection (18/20)

What

  • A 7-compartment pill tray with an automated dispenser, built to cut down on missed doses.
  • Combines a real-time clock, display and Raspberry Pi Pico into one mechatronic device.
  • Reminds the user and confirms both dispensing and retrieval.

How

  • Mechanical: a 3D printed rotating carousel driven by a unipolar motor, advancing one compartment per day.
  • Sensors: an IR break-beam confirms the pill drop, an ultrasonic sensor tracks the tray, and a PIR sensor detects the user.
  • Power: a DC-DC buck converter regulates 5V, and the Pico runs components from its 3.3V rail.

Results

  • 90% accuracy in pill-drop detection (18 of 20). Both misses traced to break-beam sensor instability.
  • The electronics performed reliably for scheduled dispensing.
  • Not as light or compact as planned. Next steps: a custom PCB and a circular housing.

Wall Mounted Hanger

MaterialAluminum
ProcessesLathe, mill
Fastener#10-24 flat head

What

  • A heavy-duty aluminum wall-mount hanger from two machined parts: a milled 2.375" × 3.250" plate and a lathe-turned peg, joined with a #10-24 flat-head screw.
  • Designed to carry real load, with an engraved, deburred finish on both visible faces.
  • Full SolidWorks drawings with GD&T and machining callouts so the parts can be made again.

How

  • Lathe part: turned 0.750" O.D. stock to a 0.502" shoulder, then faced, chamfered 45°, countersunk, tapped #10-24 and parted to 5/8".
  • Mill part: squared the stock, tapped the center hole, cut two 0.25"-radius cutouts, planed to 0.360" and countersunk four corner holes.
  • Verification: checked thread and countersink fits, then deburred and engraved.

Results

  • Measured 2.367" × 3.255" × 0.3625" against targets of 2.375", 3.250" and 0.360". All within a few thousandths.
  • The parts mated cleanly on the first assembly attempt.
  • Next steps: reduce wall thickness to save weight and add a protective coating.

Mini Mailbox

Material24 ga steel, hardwood
ProcessesSheet metal, woodworking
Tolerance±1/16"

What

  • A miniature hinged-door mailbox from two subsystems, sheet metal and wood, all made by hand.
  • Toleranced hand drawings and repeatable fabrication procedures.

How

  • Sheet metal: sheared, punched and filed the roof, door and back from steel. Hand-formed the door arch, then arc-bent the roof to a matching radius.
  • Wood: cut the base and bottom, notched blind holes for the hinge tabs and chamfered both parts.
  • Assembly: bent the hinge tabs, drilled the shell holes, screwed it together and glued in the dowel.

Results

  • Held almost every dimension to ±1/16", including a roof bend radius that nearly matches the back’s arch.
  • A working mailbox with a hinged door that opens and closes.
  • Next steps: even out the roof’s bend radius and adjust tolerances so the door moves more smoothly.
